Sunflowers are among my favourite flowers. At this size, we can observe them clearly. Sunflowers are typically pollinated by bees. However, when no bees are present, they have a clever mechanism to ensure pollination occurs. The male stamen curls inward to deposit pollen onto the sticky female stigma. This process is referred to as self-pollination. In plants such as Ixora, the anther and the stigma reach maturity at the same time. This simultaneous maturation of the anther and the stigma facilitates self-pollination, a process known as homogamy. Some plants, like wood sorrel during late summer, produce small and unnoticeable flowers that lack fragrance. These bisexual flowers remain closed. The occurrence of self-pollination and fertilization within an unopened flower is called clistogamy. In such plants, the pollen grain is already distributed on the stigma of the same flower to enable self-pollination. Cross-pollination Papaya is a unisexual, dioecious plant. Male and female flowers grow on separate plants and typically undergo average cross-pollination. However, sunflowers have bisexual flowers. In these flowers, the anthers mature first, followed by the stigma. This process is known as protandry. On the other hand, in custard apple, the stigma matures before the anther. This process is referred to as protogyny. Some flowers, such as pansies, have a hood that covers the stigma. This structural adaptation is called harkogamy. In flowers like orchids, the pollen from the anther fails to germinate on the stigma of the same flower. Primrose exhibits heterostyly, where the stigma is located at a different height compared to the anther. These are various adaptations in flowers that aid cross-pollination. What draws a pollinator to a flower? Sometimes it's the sweet scent and the promise of nutritious nectar. Other times, it's deception. A flower known as the bee orchid mimics the appearance of a female bee. This flower attracts male bees seeking a mate. When these males attempt to mate with the flower, they end up pollinating it.
