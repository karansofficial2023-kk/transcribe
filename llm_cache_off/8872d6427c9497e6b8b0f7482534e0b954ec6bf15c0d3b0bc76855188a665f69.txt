But what exactly powers these batteries, and how are they made? Exploring this inside a phone could be quite fascinating. Most modern mobile devices rely on lithium-ion batteries. These use solid electrolytes that allow ions to travel from the negative electrode to the positive electrode during discharge and return when charging. Lithium-ion cells can be recharged many times and are categorized as secondary cells. In contrast, the dry cell found in your wall clock or toys, which eventually stop working after extended use, are classified as primary cells. Batteries that utilize lead storage or nickel cadmium also lose power over time but can be recharged again.
